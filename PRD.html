UtsavPlan: Product Requirements Document

Working name. Event planning and coordination for Indian family functions. Draft for the buildathon.

Pitch line: "They help you find vendors. We help you pull off the event."

1. Summary

UtsavPlan helps families plan and run any function: weddings, and also haldi, mehendi, engagements, birthdays, naming ceremonies and housewarmings. It combines three things in one app:

  1. Discovery. Find and book vendors, especially independent artists (mehendi artists, freelance photographers, home caterers, decorators) who today exist mostly on Instagram, WhatsApp and word of mouth.
  2. Coordination. After booking, one shared event plan for the whole family: tasks, vendors, payments made and pending, things to buy, and a day-of timeline.
  3. Trust. Reviews and proof of work come only from real, completed bookings.

2. Problem

Planning a function today means:

  • Finding vendors through calls, Instagram and word of mouth, with no reliable way to judge quality.
  • Scattered WhatsApp groups where decisions, prices and timings get lost.
  • Cash advances and no record of who has been paid, how much, and what is still pending.
  • No single place to see who is doing what and when, so family members keep asking the same questions.
  • Independent artists are hard to discover and hard to trust, so families fall back on the few people they already know.

4. Goals and non-goals

Goals

  • Let a family go from "we are planning a haldi" to a confirmed, tracked plan in one sitting.
  • Make independent artists discoverable by showing their work, not just a name and a number.
  • Give the whole family one live view of the plan.
  • Earn trust through verified bookings.
  • Cover small functions, so users come back several times a year and not once for a wedding.

Non-goals (for the MVP)

  • Real online payments, contracts, or cancellation handling.
  • In-app chat (link out to WhatsApp instead).
  • Maps, location tracking, native mobile apps.
  • Vendor self-signup (the MVP uses seeded vendors).

5. How we are different

Existing wedding apps such as WeddingBazaar and WedMeGood are mainly vendor directories. They help you find vendors and earn mostly from leads and sponsored listings. Instagram and WhatsApp are where independent artists actually live, but they offer no booking, tracking or trust layer.

Why it can win: directories stop at "found a vendor". We start there and own the rest of the journey, which creates a reason to come back and data (completed bookings) that makes trust real.

8. Key user flows

Flow A: Plan an event

  1. User taps "Plan an event" and picks one or more event types, date, city, guests and budget.
  2. The app creates the event and a recommended list: vendors needed, tasks, and things to buy.
  3. User edits the list: ticks or unticks items, adds their own.
  4. For each needed vendor category, "Find artists" opens the directory pre-filtered to that category.

Flow B: Find and book a vendor

  1. User searches or filters in the directory and previews work with "View work".
  2. User opens a profile, sees the work gallery, services and prices, and shortlists or requests a booking.
  3. The request attaches to one of the user's events (or prompts them to create one first).
  4. The booking appears on the event as Requested.

Flow C: Artist confirms, family sees it

  1. The artist opens their booking link and taps Confirm or Decline.
  2. The status changes on the event plan and on the family view within about 2 seconds.

Flow D: Run the event

  1. The organiser shares one link with the family (read-only, no login).
  2. Family tracks tasks, vendors, payments and shopping. The organiser marks payments paid and bookings Completed.
  3. (Stretch) Day-of timeline shows arrivals and ceremony times; completed bookings unlock reviews.

9. Functional requirements (MVP)

Plan SetupUser can select multiple event types. Creating an event generates needs, tasks and shopping items from templates merged across the selected types.
Event planOwner can tick, untick and add tasks and shopping items, change quantities, add payments, and mark payments paid. Changes persist and appear on the family view.

Family viewRead-only. Reachable by link without login. Refreshes every 2 seconds.
ShortlistSave and remove vendors from cards and profiles. A shortlist page lists them. Stored on the user's device.

12. Design principles

  • Mobile first. Families open the share link on their phones.
  • Minimal and warm: off-white background, charcoal text, one deep maroon accent, thin line icons, and real photos carrying the color.
  • Filters live in a side drawer so the vendor list stays clean.
  • Work is visible before any booking decision.

13. Demo and pitch outline

  1. Story. Open with a relatable chaotic family function: WhatsApp groups, cash advances, nobody sure who is coming when.
  2. Live demo. Plan a haldi, get the list, browse artists and preview their work, book one. On a second phone the artist confirms, and the family view updates live.
  3. Differentiation slide. "They help you find vendors. We help you pull off the event."
  4. Growth story. See section 14.

14. Growth plan

  1. Start with small functions and independent artists in one city.
  2. Add more event types and categories, and open vendor self-signup.
  3. Expand to more cities and languages; add payments and AI planning.

Why small functions first: they happen several times a year per family, so retention is natural, and independent artists have the most to gain from being discoverable.
